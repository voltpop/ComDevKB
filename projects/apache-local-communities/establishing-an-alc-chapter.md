## Prerequisites

- **Minimum team**: at least 1 ASF Member plus 2 PMC members in the
  proposed chapter's team. Exception: ComDev PMC may assign a mentor
  (an ASF Member) case-by-case for regions without local members to
  draw on.
- **One chapter per location** — check the
  [ALC Chapters index](https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/display/COMDEV/ALC+Chapters)
  first to confirm the city/region doesn't already have one.

## Step-by-step

1. **Submit the request.** Initiator emails `dev@community.apache.org`
   with subject `[ALC] Request to establish ALC in <location>`, listing
   proposed initial members (Apache IDs where they have one) and a
   proposed Chapter Lead.
2. **Member confirmation (7-day window).** A ComDev PMC member emails
   all named participants — cc'ing `private@community.apache.org` — to
   confirm each accepts the proposed Chapter Lead and agrees to the
   ALC roles and responsibilities.
3. **PMC vote (7 days).** ComDev PMC votes on `private@`:
   `[+1]` establish, `[0]` abstain, `[-1]` reject.
4. **Decision communicated** back on the original request thread;
   rejections come with an explanation.
5. **Wiki infrastructure created** on approval: a main chapter page
   (`ALC <Name>`), an Events page, a Reports page, a team-meeting-notes
   page, and an entry added to the
   [Reports index](https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/display/COMDEV/Reports).
6. **Handoff and mentorship**: Chapter Lead (and named members with a
   known Confluence ID) get wiki edit rights; a mentor from an existing
   chapter is assigned for the initial period; the Code of Conduct and
   [Guidelines to organise ALC Event](https://cwiki.apache.org/confluence/display/COMDEV/Guidelines+to+organise+ALC+Event)
   are handed over.
7. **Public announcement** on `announce@apache.org`.

## Ongoing obligations (the "heartbeat," per the R&R doc)

- Chapter Lead is the point of contact for the chapter.
- Chapter must follow the ALC Code of Conduct and the event guidelines.
- Chapter Lead submits a status report to `dev@` **every three months**,
  on a cycle set at establishment (e.g. ALC Budapest's cycle was
  Aug/Nov/Feb/May).
- The **ALC Chair** role (foundation-wide across all chapters, not
  per-chapter) is responsible for making sure quarterly reports actually
  get filed, and reviews pre-event materials alongside the PMC.
- Per the main ALC overview page: chapters inactive for 3+ months are
  subject to dissolution via a PMC vote — see [[overview]] for why this
  doesn't appear to be happening in practice.

## Running an event, once established

1. Create a child page under the chapter's Events page with the agenda
   and details.
2. Email `dev@community.apache.org` with an `[ALC]`-prefixed subject
   and wait for PMC approval before proceeding.
3. Run the event.
4. Post a report on the event's wiki page and share it with `dev@`.
5. Keep the chapter's Events page current (past + upcoming).
6. Sponsors must be told explicitly they're sponsoring the *event*, not
   the ASF.

## Worked example: ALC Budapest (2020)

The [Budapest thread](https://lists.apache.org/thread/cxnq8zzysc40xg3sv3nto9qx5tzt26zq)
matches this shape closely: request Feb 2020 → queued behind ALC Warsaw
→ PMC vote → approval and wiki setup (May 2020), including chapter lead
assignment, Confluence rights for named members by known ID, and a
pairing with an experienced member of ALC Indore as onboarding support
for "the first few months." Real-world gap from the same thread: the
request sat roughly three months (Feb → May) behind a queued prior
chapter before being acted on — worth keeping in mind when routing the
two pending 2026 requests noted in [[overview]].
