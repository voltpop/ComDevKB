# Every ASFU chapter runs a board, and every effort inside it runs a PMC

Drafted 2026-11-25 from Drew Foulks' answers (see the question record `../records/2026-11-25-adr-chapter-governance.md`). AI-generated by claude-code/claude-sonnet-5-5, awaiting ratification by Drew. Nothing here is ruled until ratified.

## Context and Problem Statement

ASFU is the ASF university outreach program (working name). Its chapters, called ALCUs and named for their school, need a governance structure. The purpose is to teach how the foundation works by letting students live it in a zero-stakes environment, while encouraging them to learn, use and work on FOSS. A chapter that practices the foundation's real forms can later do them for real, and the foundation gains people who already know how it runs.

Earlier notes left the structure open. The scaffold talk (2026-11-20) said "PMC-like first, board only when needed". A later conversation proposed that a chapter pick the board model if it wants a president and the PMC model if a management committee will do. Both are superseded by the decision below. See [the launch plan](../launch-plan.md) and [the scaffold talk](../talk-2026-11-20-asfu-scaffold.md).

Scope: chapters only. Program-level governance (who oversees ASFU itself, who runs the cross-chapter heartbeat) is a separate decision.

## Decision Drivers

* Teach by emulation: reuse the foundation's concepts and names so learning transfers.
* Chapter-led: students lead internally, guided by a Shepherd, not run by one.
* Teach both foundation models in one chapter: the Board and the PMC.
* Low stakes: no money, no academic credit, no legal weight.
* Leadership turnover as students graduate.
* Anyone can: roles are written holes any member can fill.
* Decisions by lazy consensus, recorded publicly.

## Considered Options

* A. Every chapter has a board, and every effort has a chapter PMC (chosen)
* B. Chapter chooses the board model or the PMC model ("does it want a president?")
* C. PMC model only, no board
* D. No prescribed structure, chapters decide

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: "A. Every chapter has a board, and every effort has a chapter PMC", because it is the only option that gives students both foundation models in one place, and it removes a choice students are not yet equipped to make.

The structure:

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Board | Every chapter has one. It runs the club. |
| Officers | The foundation's full set: President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer. The Treasurer seat teaches the role and holds no money. |
| Term and election | One academic year. Elections each fall. All chapter members vote. |
| Member | A person who joins the chapter's Discord and attends one meeting. |
| Board meetings | Monthly. The Secretary posts minutes on the chapter's page. The minutes serve as proof of a meeting. |
| Efforts | Each effort is run by a chapter PMC, written "<School> chapter PMC". Examples named so far: fundraising, outreach, education, development (perhaps for metrics). The set is open. |
| New effort | A written proposal, approved by the board by lazy consensus. |
| Chapter PMC chair | Chosen per project, no fixed term. |
| Decisions | By lazy consensus, recorded publicly. Discord is social, nothing official is decided there. |
| Shepherd | One ASF Member per chapter guides it. The Shepherd reads the monthly report and issues the friendly Roll Call when a chapter goes quiet. ComDev sees only the count of missed reports. |
| Monthly report | Three to five lines plus proof of a meeting. |

### Consequences

* Good, because students practice both the Board and the PMC in a zero-stakes setting, and meet real foundation vocabulary.
* Good, because there is one structure to document, teach and check, not a fork by chapter.
* Good, because annual elections turn leadership over as students graduate.
* Bad, because a board with four officers is heavy for a chapter of five students. A small chapter may fill seats by doubling up. This ADR does not allow vacant officer seats to block a meeting.
* Bad, because a chapter PMC with no chair term has no clock, so a chair can hold a seat indefinitely.
* Bad, because one Shepherd per chapter is a single point of failure. This is the failure the ALC heartbeat already suffers.
* Bad, because "PMC" and "board" are real foundation terms. Mixing them up with the real bodies is a risk, handled by always writing "<School> chapter PMC" and "<School> chapter board".

### Confirmation

No automated test can prove this. The human check is a review after the pilot chapters (VSU, UNCC, HU) complete one cycle: does each chapter have a board with minutes posted monthly, and at least one chapter PMC with a recorded decision? Drew reviews.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### A. Board per chapter, PMC per effort (chosen)

* Good, because it mirrors the foundation, where the Board sits over projects run by PMCs.
* Good, because it teaches both models without a choice.
* Bad, because it is the most structure to start with.

### B. Chapter chooses board or PMC by whether it wants a president

* Good, because it lets small chapters stay light.
* Bad, because chapters that pick the PMC model never practice the Board, and Drew expected most would pick the board anyway.
* Bad, because it asks students to choose a structure before they understand either.

### C. PMC model only

* Good, because it is the lightest and matches how projects are run.
* Bad, because there is no president or Board to emulate, and students expect a president.

### D. No prescribed structure

* Good, because it is the most chapter-led.
* Bad, because nothing is taught: it gives up the purpose of the program.

### Overruled recommendations

* Officer seats: the recommendation was President, Vice President and Secretary only, because a Treasurer holds no money. Overruled by Drew: the full foundation set teaches every role.
* Chapter PMC chair term: the recommendation was six months, rotating. Overruled by Drew: chosen per project.
* Shepherds: the recommendation was two per chapter. Overruled by Drew: one per chapter.
